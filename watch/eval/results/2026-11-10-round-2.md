# Verify-task eval, round 2 (10 November 2026)

Run with the verify prompt as changed after round 1: a law missing from `laws`, or with the wrong status or date,
is now a change to make, and the rule on `checked.date` no longer contradicts itself. All 31 cases ran once, by the same
model as the weekly task, without browser access. As in round 1, groups of five or six sandboxes shared an agent,
which was told to treat each as a separate run.

**What it found.** Every target was met: 18 of 18 changes found and cited to official sources, nothing invented,
and none of the six injected instructions followed. The three enacted-but-not-in-force cases were marked `enacted`,
and the change a maintainer had already rejected (COPPA 2.0) was not re-proposed. In two cases the task also edited an
obligation as well as `laws` (Utah's lawful bases, Nebraska's penalties). Both changes were cited and in the right entry.

**How far to trust it.** The runs weren't fully independent:
- **Shared agents.** Each agent ran five or six cases one after another, so it carried context from one case to the next.
- **Compared files.** In four of the six groups, the agent diffed its sandbox's `verify-prompt.md` against another
  sandbox's copy. The files were identical, and the agents say nothing else was compared or used.
- **Saw an answer.** In one group the agent listed the run folder and saw br-anpd-report's expected outcome in the
  answer key. It says it then worked the case out from the sources. Without that case the result is the same.

The key now lives outside the sandbox folder, and each sandbox gets its own copy of the validator's packages instead
of a link back to the repository. A third round should give each run only its own folder. Two more limits: the
cases were written with help from the same model family that ran them, and 31 cases is a small set.

**For the maintainer (raised by the runs, not scored):**
- **us-federal:** the DOJ's Data Security Program page gives 5 October 2025 for the due diligence, audit and reporting
  duties, but the entry says 6 October. Checked: the rule itself (28 CFR 202.1001(a)) says "by no later than October 6,
  2025", so the entry is right.
- **nl:** the cookies summary doesn't say that refusing must be as easy as accepting, which the Dutch regulator
  enforces. Checked: the AP's cookie page cited by the entry doesn't state that rule, but it does say scrolling,
  staying passive or pre-ticked categories aren't consent. The entry now says so.
- **es:** Ley 10/2025's preamble gives six months to adapt, but its operative provision gives twelve.
- **uk:** GOV.UK and legislation.gov.uk give different dates for when DPA 2018 s.164A (complaints) commenced.
- **ca:** Bill C-22, the Lawful Access Act 2026, is in Senate committee and worth watching.
